I feel like I haven't written anything here for a while, about 2 months to be exact. It's been a combination of burnout and being busy. But I'm back now, and ready to share some updates on what I've been up to and where this website is going moving forward.

## What I've been up to (and why I went quiet)

As always, I was busy hacking the world, playing with "toys", and building stuff for hackers to hack.

A huge chunk of the last two months was spent volunteering with the [Radio Frequency Hackers Sanctuary (RFHS)](https://rfhackers.com/RF-Hackers-Sanctuary/) on their Capture The Flag challenges, specifically the fox hunting challenges. That meant a lot of low-level C++ coding on microcontrollers (Nano ESP32, Seeed Studio C5, etc.) and building a few new foxes for their challenges. For my contributions, I earned a challenge coin at [DEF CON 34](https://defcon.org/html/defcon-34/dc-34-index.html) this year, which was a special moment I'll always remember. I definitely want to write a post about fox hunting and all this when I have some time. There are so many things I could write about now that it's actually getting harder to pick one topic lol.

On top of that, I was juggling a full-time job, doing a lot of RFID hacking lately, and messing around with some cool new toys I picked up. I also wrote and published a research paper as the lead author alongside one of my friends and two of his friends, and then presented it at a conference. First time writing and presenting a paper, and it went really well. I might do more of that in the future.

Conclusion: I was busy. But I was also burnt out from writing and maintaining this website. I've learned a lot about running a technical blog since I started it. Writing the posts, designing and maintaining the site itself, and preparing tons of screenshots for every article was taking way too much time away from the hacking I actually enjoy doing. Back when I was on Jekyll, I wasn't using AI for the site or the posts at all. It was just me reading docs and figuring everything out by hand. Things would randomly break and I'd have to go figure out why, fix it, and document it in my little incident doc plan for the site. It was a lot.

Like I said though, I've learned a lot from that, and I'm finally ready to level up a bit. I already started by moving from Jekyll to Hugo & Blowfish, and there's more on the way.

## Why this site exists

Before getting into what's changing, it's worth mentioning why I started this site earlier this year in 2026. I do a lot of hacking and breaking stuff (and now a lot of building/dev work too), so I wanted a place to document my adventures. Somewhere I can always go back, relive those projects, and recheck commands, tools, hiccups, and challenges I ran into along the way. It turned out to be a great conversation starter with tech people too, even if that wasn't why I built it.

I like to think of this website as a photo album, but for my projects. My projects and posts are the photos, and this site is my album. Sometimes I sit down and read my old posts and think, *"wow, I really did this?"* or *"I can't believe I did it this way, that was not very efficient, I could have done it like this instead."* It's genuinely fun, it helps me learn about myself, and I can see myself grow through every post in how I build and write.

I'm always glad when people share my posts, and I love getting new ideas, feedback, and discussions on these topics. At the end of the day though, I built this for myself first, and everything shared here is my own thoughts. Anybody is free to disagree. Heck, I disagree with myself sometimes when I read old posts, and that's how it should be.

## Where this website is going next

First, I've decided to start using AI to help maintain this website so I can focus more on hacking and writing. At work, I use AI quite a lot to build tools and automate stuff, so it just sorta makes sense to use it here too. It saves a lot of time when you know how to steer it, though it's definitely not perfect and will still make random decisions on its own if left unsupervised, even after clear instructions not to do a specific thing because it's inefficient and stupid. I could go on and on about AI, but the point is it saves time. It's also a good excuse to practice using it outside my usual work, and I'm thinking of having it write some posts as well just to see how much better the models have gotten; call it a little experiment. I already started with the [Jekyll to Hugo post](/posts/porting-the-site-to-hugo/) (and I'll keep labeling AI-written posts clearly when I do).

Another change is how often I post. I'm not officially a member of the RFHS, but they're kind of like my family and I want to keep working with them because it's fun and they're great people. I love what they do for the hacker community and want to keep contributing. We all volunteer doing something, so I guess I found mine? Time will tell. That means less free time to write, so I'll be more selective about which technical projects I turn into full write-ups, and there might be a few more general blog posts mixed in than before.

I'll also include fewer images in my posts moving forward. They look great and make posts nicer to read, but maaannn, the amount of work is astronomical. I don't got time like that lol. So fewer images and more code blocks instead. It's just easier that way. Some of my favorite and most visual posts, [P25 Scanner](/posts/p25-scanner/) and [Pentoo Installation](/posts/pentoo-installation/), are packed with images, and they took forever to put together. Coincidentally (as expected), those were my very first posts. I've definitely come a long way since then.

Additionally, I'll be doing some cleanup on the site sometime after this post: tidying up the categories, updating the About me section, and a few other tweaks. None of this is about winding the blog down; it's just cutting out the busywork so writing stays fun. There's still plenty on my list I'm excited to dig into here, from RFHS fox hunting and microcontroller builds to more RFID rabbit holes whenever I get the time.

That's pretty much it. That's all I felt like talking about today. Tomorrow, I'll wake up and feel like crap: *"I should have talked about this and that, I forgot to mention XYZ"*... aren't we all humans lol? But nope, that's it. No more editing this piece. See ya.
